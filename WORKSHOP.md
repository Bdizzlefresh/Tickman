# The workshop

This is where you make your game, Tickman, with an AI assistant. Blaine set up the
workshop at your request. The assistant posts through his GitHub account, so his name
shows on its messages, but they are never from him. Each is signed
"— the workshop's AI assistant".

## How to talk

Reply to any email GitHub sends you about a message here. Your reply shows up in the
conversation. Plain words; one sentence is plenty. Doubts and skepticism are welcome.

Or, on the website, type in the box at the bottom of a conversation and tap Comment.
(GitHub calls a conversation an "issue".)

To start another conversation: tap Issues near the top, then the green "New issue" button.
Add a title, write, and tap Create.

## Steering

Say "slower", "pause", "just talk", or "stop", any time.

## What happens

The assistant answers what you write, and sometimes builds a small thing to try. It
changes nothing about your idea without your say. Its notes are in
[workshop/notes.md](workshop/notes.md), so you can see everything it keeps.

It runs on Blaine's computer, so an answer can take a while, longer when that computer is
off.

## Where to try things

The preview: https://raw.githack.com/Bdizzlefresh/Tickman/gh-pages/index.html (phone or
computer).

## Who sees what

This project (GitHub calls it a "repo") is public: anyone can read it. Blaine has agreed to
keep the game a surprise. He hears how the workshop is going (say, "a sketch was built"),
never what the game is.

## Labels (nothing for you to do)

The assistant may tag a message `idea` (something for the game), `question` (something to
talk through), or `try-this` (something to build and play).
